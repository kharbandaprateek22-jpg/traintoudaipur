CHAPTER 01

An Unexpected Journey

October 16, 2026 · Delhi

It was an ordinary evening in Delhi.

I had been living in Dubai for quite some time, working as a network architect. Most of my days revolved around work, meetings, network designs, and the endless stream of things that demanded my attention.

Perhaps that was why, when I had to travel to Udaipur, I wanted to do it differently.

I could have taken a flight. I could have driven there. Both options would have been easier and considerably faster.

But I wanted to take a train.

I missed the railway platforms, the sound of vendors calling out for passengers, the smell of chai, the rhythmic clatter of wheels against the tracks, and unfamiliar landscapes passing by the window.

I had no idea that among all those passengers, I was about to meet someone who would make this otherwise ordinary journey unforgettable.

Coach B6. Seat number 34.

A middle berth.

And a journey that would change the way I remembered those three days.

CHAPTER 02

A Face in the Moonlight

Across from me, on the lower side berth, two girls were trying to sleep.

That was how our conversation began.

At first, it was the usual exchange between two strangers. Where are you from? What do you do? Where are you going?

There was something easy about the conversation. Neither of us seemed particularly interested in ending it.

Then, around four in the morning, she mentioned that she was hungry.

That was when I remembered the food my mother had packed for me before I left home.

Aloo gobi and parathas.

And there we were, at four in the morning, sharing a home-cooked meal aboard a moving train.

Then the breeze caught her hair.

As her hair moved away from her face, the moonlight fell on it, revealing her features properly for the first time.

I looked at her.

And for a moment, I forgot what I was about to say.

I didn't know it then, but I had arrived in Udaipur with something more than a travel plan.

I had arrived with the memory of a face I had seen in the moonlight.

CHAPTER 03

A City of Lakes and Unspoken Thoughts

I started with Fateh Sagar Lake.

I spent some time there before heading to the City Palace. After that, I made my way toward Lake Pichola.

It was somewhere between eight and nine in the evening when I noticed her at the rooftop restaurant.

She was wearing a yellow floral top. Her hair fell beautifully around her face.

Then, around half past ten, my phone lit up.

It was her.

Hey Prateek, wanna go for a quick smoke?

I smiled at the message.

Sure, why not?

The following morning, I left for Bahubali Hills alone. I watched the sunrise and returned to the hotel.

I had a beautiful city to explore, but somewhere in my thoughts, one face kept appearing.

CHAPTER 04

A Face That Followed Me

When I reached Pipliya Village, I found myself surrounded by what looked like an endless stretch of forest.

After a while, I turned back and eventually made my way toward Rayta Hills.

The road wound through the hills, rising and falling as it curved through the landscape.

But for some reason, no matter where the road took me, one face kept appearing in my thoughts.

Her face.

Her eyes.

The way her hair had moved in the breeze that night.

That evening, we met again near Fateh Sagar Lake.

We talked, laughed, and shared a little more of our lives.

There was something wonderfully effortless about moments like these.

We were simply two people sharing a strange little experience in an unfamiliar city.

CHAPTER 05

One More Glimpse

That night, I had the feeling that perhaps neither of us was in a hurry to leave.

I offered her another cigarette.

She accepted.

So we stayed a little longer.

I don't know if she wanted to spend more time with me, or if she simply felt like having another cigarette.

All I knew was that I was happy to have a little more time with her.

Before leaving Udaipur, I wanted to see her face one more time.

I don't know why.

I wanted to capture that glimpse in my memory, to carry it with me after the journey was over.

Perhaps I knew, even then, that I might never get another chance to see her.

And I wasn't ready for the journey to end.

EPILOGUE

Three Days,
A Lifetime of Memories.

Her last day in Udaipur had passed.

By then, she would probably have boarded her train and started moving away from the city, carrying her bags, her own memories, and whatever the journey had meant to her.

I was still there.

That night, I went outside my hotel for one last cigarette.

Alone.

I thought about the train, the blue lights, and the first time I had seen her face in the moonlight.

I didn't need to know whether we'd meet again to be grateful that we had met at all.

Not every beautiful experience needs to become a permanent part of our lives.

Not every person we meet is meant to stay.

And not every connection needs a name to have meant something.

Three days in which a stranger became a memory.
Three days in which a city became a feeling.

I had come to Udaipur expecting to discover a city.

I hadn't expected to discover a memory that would stay with me long after I left it behind.

Some journeys end when we reach our destination.
Others stay with us long after we've returned home.

And this one?

This one will always be
Train to Udaipur.
